Extension of Revenue Signal · 90-second case

Google changed how Local Services campaigns are typed. A budget decision should not change with it.

In 2026 Google is moving Local Services Ads into Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, and pest control is in the first wave. If a pipeline still sorts spend into channels the old way, that spend lands under Search. Nothing errors. ChangeGuard catches it, refuses to trust the budget signal built on it, and repairs history without counting anything twice.

1 · The change

What Google documents, and what it does not.

Verified
Existing Local Services campaigns migrate automatically to a Performance Max type with pay-per-lead goals. Phase 1 started August 2026 for some U.S. businesses, including pest control.support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585
Verified
Local Services performance reports do not transition to Google Ads, and the old dashboard becomes inaccessible. Past lead records do transfer.support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585
Verified
In the Google Ads API, a migrated campaign is PERFORMANCE_MAX with pmax_campaign_settings.local_services_enabled = true, not LOCAL_SERVICES.developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/campaigns/local-service-campaigns (updated 2026-10-30)
Inferred
The migrated campaign gets a new campaign id, since a campaign's channel type cannot change. Google does not say so directly.
Hypothetical
A pipeline that classifies channel by platform or by LOCAL_SERVICES files the migrated spend as Google Search. That is the failure this demo engineers.
2 · The symptom

Local Services spend stops on October 13. Its leads do not.

Google Local ServicesGoogle Search

Total spend at the branch did not change, so the branch scorecard looks normal. The money moved between channels: from Local Services to Search.

Local Services leads kept arriving after the spend stopped. A channel with leads and no cost is the signature of a classification break, not of free customers.

A cross-system rule caught it at the daily run on : a paid channel keeps producing leads while its spend stops, and a new campaign starts in the same ad account.

3 · The evidence

Two stored records, one day apart, filed in different channels.

Same ad account, same business, new campaign id and type. Mapper ads@1.0 reads the platform and nothing else.

4 · The gate

The budget signal is shown with what makes it untrustworthy.

The rule is deliberately simple and labelled as such: flag a paid channel that costs at least 1.5× another per new customer. It stands in for whatever person or model acts on channel cost.

As reported, Search looks × as expensive as Local Services. That would justify a large shift. While the coverage gap is open, the signal is marked not trustworthy and links to the incident.

After the re-map, the gap is real but much smaller: ×. Local Services still looks cheaper here, so a shift may still be right. It is now a shift sized on correct numbers.

The gate blocks on incidents that change what a channel cost or which customers it produced: coverage gaps, held source records, spend nobody has mapped.

5 · The repair and the proof

Re-map the stored records. Move classification, never money.

Mapper ads@1.1 reads the campaign type and the local-services flag. A re-projection replay re-maps the stored records, claims each record once, records the campaign lineage, and recomputes every number from the database.

6 · Verified by tests

What the automated tests hold it to.

  • IdempotentThe same key returns the stored result; a new key finds nothing left to claim; a reused key for a different request is refused.
  • ConcurrentFour re-maps racing on real Postgres claim each record exactly once.
  • Fails safeAn injected database failure mid-replay rolls everything back; the same request then succeeds.
  • Refuses to guessA record the new mapper cannot read keeps its earlier projection instead of being reclassified.
  • History survivesA post-migration re-pull that no longer returns the old Local Services rows deletes nothing.
  • Continuous and conservedAfter the re-map, Local Services spend exists for all 60 days; spend, customers, invoices and payments are unchanged across all 44 branches.
